UNDERSTANDING REIKI

A quiet space.
Care for your whole self.

Reiki is a Japanese complementary healing practice offered through gentle touch or hands held above the body. Its traditional framework centres on universal life energy and a holistic approach to body, emotions, mind and spirit. Sessions provide dedicated time for relaxation, supportive presence and personal wellbeing. Reiki is also offered within some hospital and cancer-support programmes alongside conventional care.

For me, Reiki is also about presence: making time to listen, soften the demands of the day and meet yourself with compassion. You do not need to follow a religion, have spiritual experience or know exactly what you need before coming.

What might bring you to Reiki?

People choose Reiki for many personal reasons. You might be looking for:

  • A gentle pause when everyday stress feels overwhelming.
  • A quiet, supportive space during grief, change or emotional upheaval.
  • Time to rest when you feel stretched, depleted or disconnected from yourself.
  • Spiritual reflection, grounding and a deeper relationship with your own intuition.
  • Comfort and personal support alongside appropriate care for illness or persistent pain.

Clinical studies have reported improvements in outcomes such as anxiety, comfort and quality of life in some groups. People also describe feeling calmer, rested or more connected after a session. Experiences vary, and your session is shaped around your needs rather than a promised outcome.

What does “healing” mean here?

In my work, healing includes reconnecting with yourself, finding meaning, receiving care and making room for what you feel. Reiki can be explored as complementary support alongside your usual healthcare. It does not replace diagnosis, prescribed treatment or professional mental health care.

Your session, shaped around you

You’re welcome to share what you’re experiencing, what you need support with or what you hope to receive. If you’re unsure, we can begin with a conversation and I’ll intuitively guide the session, with your permission and preferences at the centre. You remain the person who decides what feels right for you.

What to expect

We begin with a gentle conversation before you settle comfortably, fully clothed. Touch is always with your permission, and a hands-off approach is available. You can ask questions, change position, request changes or pause at any time.

You may notice warmth, tingling, emotions, quietness or very little sensation. There is nothing to force or perform, and feeling little does not mean you have done anything wrong. We finish with time to reflect and gently return to your day.

RESEARCH & HEALTHCARE

Reiki in research.
Reiki in supportive care.

Reiki has been studied in clinical trials and is offered by some hospitals as part of complementary and supportive care. There are positive findings worth recognising, alongside questions that researchers are continuing to explore.

  • Anxiety: a 2024 meta-analysis involving 824 adults reported an overall reduction in anxiety with Reiki, although findings varied across patient groups and settings. Read the review in BMC Palliative Care ↗
  • Quality of life: a 2025 meta-analysis of 11 studies involving 661 participants reported a small average improvement in quality of life. Results varied between studies, and further high-quality research was recommended. Read the review in Systematic Reviews ↗
  • Comfort during cancer treatment: a 2026 review of four studies involving 339 people with breast cancer found promising results for fatigue, comfort and wellbeing. The authors highlighted the need for larger, well-designed trials. Read the review in Supportive Care in Cancer ↗
  • Hospital use: Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center offers Reiki to cancer patients during inpatient stays and outpatient infusion visits as part of its supportive-care programme. Explore the hospital programme ↗

These findings support continued study of Reiki as a complementary approach to wellbeing and symptom support. They do not establish Reiki as a cure for disease or prove the traditional explanation of energy transfer. The strength of evidence differs by outcome, study design and type of Reiki offered; hospital use alone is not proof of effectiveness.

RECEIVE FROM YOUR OWN HOME

Distance Reiki.
The same care, wherever you are.

Distance Reiki is a way to receive a session without travelling to be in the same room. It can suit you if you live further away, find travel difficult, prefer a hands-off experience or simply feel most comfortable resting at home.

How does it work?

Distance Reiki is a non-touch form of the practice, offered through focused intention while you and I are in different locations. During our agreed session time, I centre my attention on you and offer the Reiki practice while you rest in your own space. In its traditional framework, distance work does not require physical proximity.

The phone or video connection is there so we can talk, prepare and reflect together. Within Reiki practice, it is not understood as the means by which energy is transmitted. You do not need to visualise anything, concentrate throughout or try to make something happen.

Is distance Reiki as good as an in-person session?

Within Reiki practice, distance work is regarded as a meaningful way to offer the same healing intention without physical proximity. I give your distance appointment the same dedicated time, presence and care as an in-person session. The experience is different: you receive in familiar surroundings, without hands-on touch or the need to travel.

Some people find distance sessions deeply restful or personally meaningful, while others prefer being together in person. That preference is yours. Research into distance Reiki is still developing, and evidence from in-person studies cannot automatically establish equivalent clinical effects for distance sessions. You receive the same care and dedicated attention, with room for your own individual experience.

What happens in your appointment?

  1. Connect: we begin with a phone or video conversation about your needs, intentions and preferences.
  2. Settle: find a quiet, comfortable place to sit or lie down, with your phone silenced apart from our agreed contact. Choose somewhere you can rest safely and will not be disturbed.
  3. Receive: I offer the distance Reiki practice during our arranged time. You may feel relaxed, emotional or notice little sensation; no particular response is required.
  4. Reflect: we reconnect afterwards, with time for you to share your experience and ask questions.

You do not need special equipment. A comfortable place to rest and a phone or video connection for our check-ins are enough. We agree the contact arrangements before your appointment.
